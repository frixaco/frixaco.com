Use case: ui-mockup
Asset type: one high-fidelity desktop personal portfolio homepage, new concept.
Primary request: Design a bold, beautiful, expressive NEOBRUTALIST OPEN STUDIO on an INFINITE CANVAS for Rustam, frixaco.com. A spacious-enough-to-read but richly populated landscape of working software exhibits, with striking contrasts of scale, shape, color and medium. This is a new approach: no tree, no mind-map connectors, no poster collage, no magazine, no conventional landing page. The visitor explores a studio of artifacts through pan, zoom, and click-to-focus.

The essential aesthetic challenge: expressiveness WITHOUT sacrificing readability. Every primary title and caption must be horizontal, fully visible, high contrast and unobscured. Medium-sized distinct readable headlines, roughly 28–40px at a 1600px-wide viewport; supporting text roughly 16–18px. NO squeezed type, enormous words, rotated text, cut-off text, overlapping text, or dense little paragraphs. Beautiful negative space BETWEEN exhibits but no huge unused zones. Restrained, coherent typography: one confident grotesk and one monospace, not a collage of fonts.

Make a wide 16:10 full-bleed website screenshot. Clean flat graphic design, no browser bezel, device mockup, photography of a real room, or isometric/3D viewpoint. The canvas itself is a warm mineral-gray surface. Four very different exhibits sit in an asymmetrical 2D composition, partly staggered and interleaved in space, NOT a grid of equal cards. Artifacts and their captions sit directly on the continuous canvas. No box wrapping a title, preview and description into a generic card. Terminal screens and video frames may have their natural boundaries. Surrounding canvas remains visible between objects so panning feels obvious.

Art direction: heavy black rules, decisive square edges, short hard unblurred offset shadows under TWO exhibits only. Oversized geometric color fields BEHIND the actual exhibits give each a physical presence without becoming decoration: one broad cobalt-blue angular stage behind Senmei, a compact acid-yellow type-shaped strip beneath Xport, a black terminal surface for LeTUI. Small warm vermilion selection markers. Keep overall palette controlled. Material diversity comes from the PROJECTS themselves, not random ornaments, stickers or scene illustration.

Persistent navigation is deliberately compact:
- Slim top strip: "frixaco.com" at left; "Rustam / software engineer"; right links "Index", "GitHub ↗", "Email ↗", "Résumé ↗".
- Directly below, one unobtrusive jump control "Jump to:  Tools  /  Video  /  Writing  /  About". These names represent regions of the same canvas.
- Small bottom bar: "Drag to explore · click an exhibit to focus", then zoom "− 100% +" and "Fit all".
These controls must be modest in scale, square-edged, visually robust and readable, not an app sidebar.

The exhibits:
1. LeTUI is a substantial BLACK terminal specimen slightly above and left of center. A modest sharp monochrome terminal UI with a few clear rows, an outlined component, and sparse colored terminal cells; no long code dump or fake performance statistics. It should feel like a working artifact, not a feature card. To its immediate left/below on the canvas, a clear caption "LeTUI", "A TUI library from scratch", small "Rust / TypeScript / Bun", and a small underlined "Explore →". Four crisp vermilion corner focus marks around this specimen and a hand cursor indicate it is the current focus target. No connected arrows to other exhibits.
2. Senmei is a broad video exhibit to the upper/right side, on the cobalt angular stage. Show a single wide beautiful original anime landscape frame with a slim vertical before/after comparison handle. The colors in the frame are rich but confined to the media. Beneath or beside it, distinct crisp label "Senmei", square "WIP", "Anime playback in the browser", small "WebGPU / MKV / subtitles", and "Play demo →". The heading is normal readable bold sans-serif, NOT gigantic italic typography. This artifact differs in proportions from the terminal, and its stage has an intentional stepped edge.
3. Xport is a SMALL horizontal typographic machine below-left: two open short text specimens, the first "a thread" and the second "# a thread", with a single stout arrow between them. They are not floating cards or fake browser windows. A hard black bracket aligns the transformation over a flat yellow strip. Nearby caption "Xport" / "Threads → Markdown" / "Try it →". Let the graphic take an unusual long-and-low shape, not another rectangle.
4. Harness Bench sits lower-right as three thin stacked terminal tracks, visually like a modest black ruler of live work rather than a dashboard. Each track has a small cursor and a few short readable status words. Caption "Harness Bench" / "AI coding agents, side by side" / "Explore →". No invented scores, charts, fake brand logos or complex tables.

A WRITING area is integrated in the lower-middle between the exhibits as three simple large clickable text lines with short, bold underline rules; no content frame. Heading "Building a TUI library", then "01  First principles", "02  Chasing 120fps", "03  Paint in Rust". Keep it easily readable, not an afterthought in microtype.

A small personal anchor near the upper-left reads "Rustam" and "exploring & building cool stuff", with "Blender / music / games / anime" quietly beneath. It is about as large as one caption group, never an oversized hero. This is personal and human without a fictional portrait or mascot.

Interaction concept to communicate through layout: the artifact itself is the destination. Click one and the camera gently focuses it, exposing detail at a closer zoom; pan freely between exhibits. Jump navigation provides immediate access to a region; Fit all returns home. No information popup, modal, sidebar preview, tree expansion or nested window system. Show a single normal overview state. Peripheral abstract edges of additional work may just enter the far viewport boundary, but NEVER crop any primary content label.

Composition should look authored, free and independent, like an excellent graphic designer laid out an artist-engineer's open studio. Tension, asymmetry and diverse artifact silhouettes; clear baseline alignment WITHIN each caption group. Useful density with room for the eye to rest. Every word is readable. The exhibits must feel spatially placed on a larger canvas, not packed into traditional website sections.

Avoid: node graphs, connector networks, card/bento grids, equal gutters or uniform repeated containers, chronological feed, article page, overlapping posters, huge display words, diagonal type, framed generic dashboard, decorative shapes unconnected to content, stars, stickers, grunge, dot grids, gradients, rounded corners, glassmorphism, neon glow, fake statistics and marketing copy. Generate exactly one polished website screenshot, not a moodboard or presentation diagram.
